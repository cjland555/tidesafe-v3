## What the visitor must believe

An 8-foot tide on Dec 24 is plausible, it would put water into my yard, and someone can measure
exactly where mine gets in before then.

One action, one label everywhere: **Request an assessment**.

## Grammar: Field report (new)

Chaptered editorial was the nearest of the eight, but it bans media above the fold, and the
standing hero rule asks for a layered photographic hero. Field report keeps the chapters and
their hard cuts and adds a photographic title plate.

- **Nav:** a thin top bar (logo, phone, the CTA). The tide gauge in the left margin is the
  real chrome: it is the reading, the chapter folio and the progress, in one object.
- **Sequence:** hard cuts between two grounds (harbor-night navy and fog), never a gradient
  drift. Each chapter opens with a dated field-note line.
- **Hero:** layered plate from the 7.0 ft photo. House behind, headline in the middle plane,
  the water cut out as a front plane that rises over the fence and swallows the bottom of the
  headline as you scroll.
- **Close:** the request form on the dark ground, with the gauge settling on a mark labeled
  "Your home: ? ft". Sources and service area set as a small colophon after it.
- **Bans:** drift, magnet, spotlight, video scrub, scroll cues, section counters, cards in a
  three-up grid, centred body copy, coral and brass.

Why the others lost: filmic one-shot (no continuous footage, and the brief asked for chapters);
live surface and gallery (nothing to operate or browse); continuous world (needs generated
footage, ruled out); typographic poster (the photos are the evidence); split stage (only the
peak is a before/after, not the whole page); rhythmic cutlist (wrong energy for a safety
decision).

## Signature move: the tide staff

A harbor tide staff fixed in the left margin, 4 to 9 ft, with two real marks: **Jul 22: 6.97 ft**
and the **Dec 24 range: 7.5 to 8.0 ft** (NOAA 7.0 plus the 6 to 12 inch Kelvin wave). The
visitor's scroll raises the water on it. During the peak it locks to the photo on screen, so
the gauge and the house agree. At the close it rests at 8.0 and adds one more mark, "Your home:
? ft", which is the question the assessment answers. On phones it becomes a slim water bar under
the header with the reading.

## Journey, feeling curve, devices

| Chapter | Beat | Feeling, and what causes it | Device |

| Title plate | Recognition | Low unease: a familiar yard with water already at the fence; the tide rises over the headline | layered parallax |

| 4. Why TideSafe | Trust | Warmth: Brett's family, 38 years on the harbor, the demo video | flow + media column |
| 5. Assessments | Choice | Control: two clear options, asymmetric, and three steps | in (stagger) |
| Questions | Reassurance | Calm: plain answers | flow (details) |
| Close | Commitment | Resolve: the form, the gauge resting on "Your home: ? ft" | in |

**Peak:** "You scroll and the water climbs the fence of a real house, all the way to 8 feet,
and then the sandbags hold." Lives in chapter 3, ~4.5 viewport-heights, the largest span.

**Tell-someone sentence:** "It's the site where you raise the tide yourself and watch exactly
where it gets into a house like yours."

**Authored silence:** the one-line dark beat before chapter 3. Not dead scroll.

## Design floor

- Type: Archivo (display, UI) and Source Serif 4 (prose). Two families.
- Grounds: harbor night `#071A2C` and fog `#EEF3F4`. Accent is the logo teal, one hue,
  two stops: `#2DC2CF` on dark, `#02708A` on light.
- Real numbers only: NOAA predictions, the July 22 reading, the Kelvin range, prices.
